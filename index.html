What it does

Three things a balance screen does not: the card as it actually behaves, the statement read on the phone, and the redemption valued after the fees came out.

💳

Your cards, as they actually behave

Add each card with its limit, statement close day, due day, annual fee and fee-waiver condition. Then describe how it earns: base rate, category accelerators, monthly caps, milestone thresholds. Cracker applies those rules to every transaction, so the points figure on screen is derived from the card in your wallet rather than from an average.

📄

Statement import, on the phone

Bank statements arrive password-protected. Cracker builds the password from details you enter once — name, date of birth, card digits — and tries them against the file, on the device. The PDF is read, parsed, reconciled against the totals the bank printed, and staged for review. The ledger is not touched until you have seen the verdict, and a committed statement can be undone whole.

Six issuers are supported today: HDFC, Axis, ICICI, IndusInd, HSBC and Bank of Baroda. Anything else can still be logged by hand.

🎁

Redemptions, valued after fees

Log what you redeemed, what it cost in points, and what it actually returned in rupees — minus booking fees and taxes. Cracker blends those into a realized value per point, weighted by points, per programme. A ₹9,600 airline transfer costing ₹1,180 in fees is ₹0.84 a point, not ₹0.96. That number is derived from your history and is never typed in.

Points retire oldest-first, so the expiry runway reports the date that is actually next, not the date a single blended balance would suggest.

Where your data lives

On this device, encrypted, and nowhere else

There is no account, no sign-in and no server. The ledger is a SQLCipher-encrypted database whose key is generated on your phone and held by the Android Keystore, so the file is unreadable to anything that copies it off — a backup tool, a file manager, another app. Your identity details live in Keystore-backed secure storage, not in the database at all, and derived statement passwords are never stored.

The release build declares exactly one permission that grants anything — USE_BIOMETRIC, which grants nothing but the fingerprint prompt itself. Notifications and boot-completed are requested only if you switch reminders on. The full account is in the privacy policy.

Because nothing is uploaded, nothing can be recovered for you. If the phone is lost, reset, or the app is uninstalled, the data is gone unless you wrote a backup file yourself and remember its passphrase. Nobody holds a copy. Nobody can reset that passphrase.
